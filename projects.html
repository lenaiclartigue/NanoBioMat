Research projects

Institutional information and general research directions across current and recent projects.

MAGNETIMA (new tab) PDF

MAGNETIMA aims to develop magnetic nanoassemblies for the treatment of autoimmune hepatitis. The project investigates magnetically controlled drug release and antigen-specific immunotherapy, combining materials development with evaluation in human cellular models and a preclinical model of the disease.

SEMAPHORE

SEMAPHORE investigates magnetic and photoactive nanoassemblies for combined photodynamic and photothermal approaches to cancer research. The project brings together expertise in nanoparticle assembly, photosensitizer chemistry and biological evaluation to study how these complementary light-activated approaches can work together.

NAMASTHEP (new tab)

NAMASTHEP investigates multifunctional magnetic nanoassemblies as a preclinical approach to hepatocellular carcinoma. The project combines formulation research with studies in liver cell models and a preclinical tumour model to evaluate biological responses and the contribution of magnetic hyperthermia.

NanoCURQMAG (new tab)

NanoCURQMAG brings together CEISAM and CR2TI to investigate curcumin-based magnetic nanoassemblies for liver disease research. The project examines their stability in biological environments and their biodistribution, with preclinical studies targeting autoimmune hepatitis and hepatocellular carcinoma.

NanoSuperSpin3D (new tab)

NanoSuperSpin3D investigates how interactions between magnetic nanoparticles influence the collective behaviour of three-dimensional nanoassemblies. The project aims to relate particle organization to magnetic response and heating under alternating magnetic fields, with potential applications in magnetic hyperthermia and imaging.

SUPERFERATITE (new tab)

SUPERFERATITE investigates biodegradable magnetic nanoassemblies for research into autoimmune liver disease. The project connects nanoparticle design and magnetic heating with studies of biological interactions and immune responses, bringing together materials chemistry and liver immunology.
