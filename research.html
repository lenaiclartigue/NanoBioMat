NanoBioMat / Research

Research

How do the characteristics of a nanoparticle and its organization with neighbouring particles determine its behaviour? How do these properties change in a biological environment?

The NanoBioMat team explores these questions by connecting nanoparticle synthesis, assembly and characterization with the study of interactions with living systems. Our research gives equal weight to the fundamental mechanisms of nanomagnetism and the design of nanoobjects for imaging and therapeutic approaches.

01

Particles — Designing properties through synthesis

Why change nanoparticle size and shape?

Because their response to a magnetic field depends on their dimensions, shape and structure.

We synthesize iron oxide nanoparticles with controlled sizes and morphologies. In particular, we seek to tune their magnetic anisotropy: the dependence of their magnetic energy on the direction of magnetization.

By relating synthesis conditions, structure and magnetic measurements, we investigate how these characteristics influence magnetization and its dynamics. Surface chemistry also allows us to control particle dispersion and prepare their assembly.

Thermal decomposition synthesis builds on work including Sun & Zeng, 2002 (new tab) and Hou et al., 2007 (new tab) on FeO synthesis and chemical conversions.

02

Assemblies — Understanding collective behaviour

What happens when nanoparticles are brought together?

They interact: the response of the assembly can differ from that of dispersed particles.

We prepare nanoassemblies that bring together multiple nanoparticles and organic molecules. We vary their composition and formation conditions to investigate their internal organization.

Interparticle distance, orientation and assembly compactness are central to our research. We seek to understand how these parameters govern magnetic interactions, magnetization dynamics and energy dissipation under alternating fields. This knowledge also supports the development of imaging and magnetic activation functions.

We use flash nanoprecipitation (Johnson & Prud’homme, 2003 (new tab)), with a CIJ setup adapted from Han et al., 2012 (new tab).

03

Biological interfaces — Understanding fate and exploring functions

What happens to nanoassemblies in contact with living systems?

They encounter proteins and cells that can modify their surface, organization and properties.

With our partners, we investigate cellular uptake, transformations and degradation. We seek to understand how composition and coatings influence these processes and biological responses.

This research supports the development of nanoassemblies for imaging, delivery of active molecules and approaches activated by magnetic fields or light. Our projects include cancer and the modulation of immune responses in liver diseases, with evaluation in cellular and preclinical models.
